OPEN METHODOLOGY

How a record is selected.

A friendly explanation of a research rule that is quite different from the fastest lap of a race. No hidden rankings.

01

Separate physical layouts

Different circuit configurations are treated separately, even if the venue name is identical. Uncertain boundaries are flagged, not silently merged.

02

Prioritize full lap distance

Within a comparable layout, find the maximum eligible completed winner lap count. Races with fewer laps are removed from the winning-time comparison.

03

Select the shortest elapsed time

Among eligible races matching that lap count, the smallest total winning time wins. Abbreviated races and special cases are audited separately.

DEMONSTRATION

Why this decision?

Race C wins: it completed the maximum 70 laps and its total winning time is shorter than Race A. Race B's 60 laps are below the comparable maximum.
Not the same as

Fastest race lap — one single lap during a race.
Fastest qualifying lap — one lap from a qualifying session, not necessarily the pole lap.

What does the evidence label mean?

Direct evidence

Contemporary or first-hand report / race schedule evidence supports the expected distance.

Fallback accepted

No independently proven advance schedule, but the lap-distance rule is met after checking available evidence for contradictions. Not a direct documentary confirmation.

Needs review

Physical layout, race geometry, race format or the published total remains unsettled.

No eligible / excluded

A race may be shortened, below the layout's maximum qualifying lap count, outside project scope, or slower than another eligible result.

Sources & data limits

Data coverage: 1,165 championship race records through 4 October 2026; 198 provisional reconciled circuit layouts. The source dataset includes F1DB, results links and historical evidence citations where available.

Historical scope: Indianapolis 500 championship rounds in 1950–1960 are retained in the audit but excluded from this F1-spec leaderboard. 1959 AVUS is an exceptional two-heat event.

Offline limitations: Search, filters, exports, favorites and explanations work entirely offline. Clicking a primary source URL requires the internet. This is an independent research project, not an official F1 publication.
